# Plan: School-band lesson platform (working name: lesson-platform)
_Locked via grill — by Claude + Josh. Revised after Codex review rounds 1–4._

## Goal
A SaaS platform for private music lesson teachers in the school band/orchestra ecosystem. The wedge: enter through **band directors**, who already run informal private-lesson recommendation lists via paper forms and email. A director spins up a free hub — explicitly the *director's personal recommendation roster*, not a school product — with one shareable sign-up link; every teacher on the roster gets a media-rich public profile; parents register and pay through the platform.

**Novelty claim (falsifiable, staged):** the nearest vertical competitor, CutTime, is director-side school-admin software (inventory, forms, finances) that districts buy; MyMusicStaff/Opus1 are teacher-side studio admin; Lesson Console is solo-studio websites. None gives a lesson teacher a public growth surface distributed *through* the director channel. Claim is tested in two stages: the **v1 gate** tests only director-assisted roster conversion (narrow claim); the **expansion gate** tests the wider claim that the teacher-owned profile generates growth beyond the school relationship, via instrumented off-hub share links. If pilot interviews show directors/teachers experience this as interchangeable with CutTime or MyMusicStaff, the novelty claim fails and we stop.

Beachhead: the founder's own high-school band network. Commercialization deferred, but payments run through the platform from day one ($0 platform fee) so take-rate options stay open.

## Approach

### Phase 0 — Concierge pilot (gates the full build)
0. **Privacy + policy prerequisites (blocking, before any data collection).**
   a. *Data-protection record* for the launch jurisdiction covering the pilot spreadsheet AND future v1: every child-related field collected, minimization rationale, who can access it, retention period, deletion/export path, subprocessors (Stripe, Vercel, Neon, video hosts), consent capture point, breach procedure. Parents own all data submissions; students under 13 never have accounts; no student names/faces on public surfaces.
   b. *Campus/district policy check, documented, two-stage*: either an applicable published district/campus policy (cited) or written confirmation from an **authorized administrator** (principal or fine-arts director — not the band director's own attestation), presented with a **one-page disclosure covering both Phase 0 and planned v1 data flows** (accounts, aggregate director analytics, Connect payments). A **second documented approval** is required before the v1 director dashboard and payments go live for that campus's families. At beachhead each approval is one email to one administrator; if either fails or goes unanswered, that is a Phase 0/v1 negative result — re-wedge, don't work around.
1. **Concierge run.** Static one-page teacher roster (hand-built profiles + click-to-load video embeds), a minimal registration form writing to a private store, coordinator spreadsheet, teacher-owned Stripe Payment Links. One real registration wave with the beachhead director. Collect: does the director distribute it, do parents use it, do teachers see sign-ups, stated willingness to pay, perceived difference vs CutTime/MyMusicStaff, and off-hub interest (do teachers ask to share their page elsewhere?).
   **Pre-validation build allowed:** public teacher profile pages (static-data driven) + the minimal registration form — nothing else. **Prohibited until the Phase 0 gate passes:** auth/accounts, memberships, director dashboard, Stripe Connect, ledger. Also during Phase 0, the three-part payments verification (see step 6): (a) sandbox technical verification of the Connect account configuration and direct-charge flow; (b) source-backed tax determination recorded in the decision record (Stripe docs citations — sandbox cannot prove 1099 behavior); (c) professional tax/legal review before the first live payment.

### v1 — Core product (builds only after Phase 0 gate passes)
2. **Data model & auth.** Next.js (App Router) on Vercel, Neon Postgres. Canonical `User` with scoped memberships — no role enum: `ProgramMembership` (user↔hub, capacity: owner/coordinator), `TeacherAffiliation` (teacher↔hub, status: invited/self-reported/confirmed, time-bounded), `Household` + `GuardianStudentLink` (multiple guardians; custody-restricted visibility flag), `Enrollment` (student↔teacher↔instrument). Auth: email magic-link (single-use, ≤15-min expiry) + Google sign-in that links only to a verified-email match (no silent account merge). Tenant-scoped authorization enforced server-side on every query.
3. **Teacher profiles (public).** Bio, instruments, rates, affiliations shown as self-reported vs hub-confirmed states, with a **permanent non-affiliation disclaimer on all surfaces regardless of affiliation state** (hub confirmation means "listed by this director," never school endorsement); media showcase via **click-to-load hosted-video embeds** (youtube-nocookie / Vimeo DNT; thumbnail + explicit click before any third-party request; moderation checklist — minors visible, copyright, identifying backgrounds — before a clip goes live). No testimonials in v1. Each profile has distinguishable share links (hub-attributed vs teacher-shared) to instrument off-hub lead generation. Abuse-report link on every public page.
4. **Director hub.** Invite-only at beachhead: founder manually verifies each director; expiring single-purpose invite links. **Director-based namespaces** ("mr-smith-lessons"), no school-name namespaces, no school branding, explicit non-affiliation disclaimer. One shareable sign-up link/QR (read-only). Director dashboard shows **aggregate** enrollment counts per instrument/teacher only — no per-family payment status.
5. **Registration flow.** Hub link → pick instrument → browse roster profiles → verify parent email (before any request is accepted) → request teacher. Per-IP and per-contact throttles; duplicate-request idempotency keys. **Request state machine:** requested → accepted / declined / expired; accepted → cancelled_by_parent / withdrawn_by_teacher / completed_term. Teachers set integer capacity per instrument; acceptance decrements capacity in a transaction; uniqueness constraint on active (student, teacher, instrument); every terminal transition idempotently releases capacity. No slot-scheduling engine — scheduling logistics stay human, as they are today.
6. **Payments — decision record (verified in Phase 0, three parts).** Stripe Connect with the simplest **supported** controller tuple: **full-Dashboard connected accounts** (`controller.stripe_dashboard.type = "full"`, `controller.fees.payer = "account"`, `controller.losses.payments = "stripe"`, `controller.requirement_collection = "stripe"`) — property names to be pinned to the chosen API version during sandbox verification. **Direct charges** on the connected account: teacher is merchant of record; statement descriptor = teacher's studio name; teacher manages refunds/disputes in their own full Stripe dashboard (platform monitors); Stripe handles the teacher's tax reporting as it would any ordinary Stripe merchant — recorded as a source-backed tax determination with citations, plus professional review before first live payment (sandbox verifies technical behavior only). This tuple minimizes platform loss exposure by design. Tradeoff: heavier teacher onboarding (full Stripe account) than an Express-style flow — acceptable for 5–15 beachhead teachers; revisit at scale.
   **Invoice ownership path:** all platform invoices are initiated in the platform UI — teachers never hand-create them in their Stripe Dashboard for platform enrollments. The platform creates the Customer and Invoice on the connected account via the `Stripe-Account` header, attaches enrollment/household metadata, persists `(connected_account_id, stripe_object_id)` pairs, and sets `application_fee_amount = 0` explicitly at launch (field present from day one, so a future take-rate is a config change, not a migration). Invoices teachers create off-platform are invisible to the platform and counted as measured leakage.
   **Billing primitive (one, v1): teacher-issued monthly invoice** — mirrors the ecosystem's existing monthly-Venmo habit. Invoice states mirror Stripe exactly: **draft → open → paid | void | uncollectible**; due/overdue are derived flags; a failed payment attempt leaves the invoice open; refunded/partially-refunded status is derived from `Refund`/`CreditNote` objects, never by mutating invoice state. Teacher-initiated full/partial refunds; disputes via Stripe flow (teacher-owned); enrollment termination stops future invoices. No per-lesson charging, no auto-recurring billing in v1. Ledger-style schema: immutable `Invoice`/`LineItem`, `PaymentAttempt`, `Refund`, `CreditNote`, `Dispute`, `Payout` keyed by unique Stripe IDs with effective-dated rate snapshots — no mutable `paid` flag. Signed, idempotent webhook inbox keyed by (account, event_id); daily reconciliation against Stripe objects; dead-letter + alerting.
7. **Data boundaries.** School-context enrollment data is firewalled from any public/marketplace surface (SEO, referrals); public pages show only what a teacher self-publishes.
8. **Instrumentation.** Per-hub contribution-margin tracking (Stripe volume, infra, support time), registration-funnel analytics (no third-party trackers on pages minors visit), and share-link attribution (hub vs off-hub) from day one.

### Post-v1 (committed, sequenced)
9. **v1.1 — Review + referral engine.** Lightweight lesson-completion confirmation by the teacher (adult-confirmed; no scheduling engine required) triggers post-lesson review requests — provenance-stamped, consent-gated, no minor identities. Shareable referral links. **Gift-a-lesson: deferred indefinitely** pending jurisdiction-specific stored-value review.
10. **v1.2 — Local SEO pages** once teacher count justifies: "[instrument] lessons in [town]" pages built exclusively from self-published teacher profiles (never school-derived data).

## Key decisions & tradeoffs
- **Vertical: music lesson teachers in the school-band ecosystem.** Purpose-built wedge vs. CutTime (school admin, district-sold), MyMusicStaff/Opus1 (studio admin), Superprof/Wyzant (horizontal marketplaces). Teacher-owned growth surface distributed via directors is the differentiator; tested in stages (v1: roster conversion; expansion: off-hub growth).
- **Independent-product path with a documented policy check.** The hub is the director's personal list — director-based namespace, no school branding, no school-supplied data — AND Phase 0 requires written campus/district acceptability confirmation before distribution. Full stripping of school context was rejected: it deletes the wedge being tested; if the policy check fails at the beachhead, that is the experiment's answer.
- **"Vetted" language banned.** Roster membership ≠ clearance; districts run their own contractor clearance. Self-reported vs hub-confirmed affiliation states only; never imply background-check status.
- **Both front doors, one core object.** Teacher profile is the product; hub is a roster view. Sales energy on directors; teacher self-serve is a signup form.
- **Payments in MVP, $0 platform fee, direct charges under Connect controller config.** Keeps take-rate open without making the platform merchant of record for children's music lessons; tax/dispute/liability posture verified in sandbox before build. Free-period cost bounded and instrumented.
- **One billing primitive: teacher-issued monthly invoices.** Matches current parent habit; defers per-lesson and recurring complexity until real usage demands it.
- **No scheduling engine in v1.** Integer capacity + a full request state machine gives correctness without modeling bell schedules; band-lesson scheduling deferred.
- **Click-to-load hosted embeds over uploads.** Kills codec/malware/egress risk AND third-party tracking risk on minor-visited pages; upload pipeline is a post-v1.2 decision.
- **Two validation gates, both falsifiable.** V1 gate (8 weeks, n=1 network): 1 director hub live, ≥5 teachers with video profiles, ≥20 families registered via hub link, **≥10 paying households** (≥1 paid invoice each through Stripe), and **≥15 teacher-reported completed lessons** (pilot self-report; systematic completion confirmation ships in v1.1). Expansion gate: second unaffiliated program, ≥2 repeat billing cycles, measured director time savings, stated price acceptance, **and the precommitted off-hub novelty threshold: within 16 weeks after the v1 gate, ≥30% of active teachers have ≥1 attributed off-hub lead, ≥10 total attributed off-hub leads, ≥3 converted to paid enrollment — the growth-platform thesis passes only if ALL THREE are met; missing any one fails it.** On failure, continuation as a roster-conversion tool is permitted only if the precommitted roster-only novelty test passes: (a) pilot interviews confirm the product is experienced as materially different from CutTime/MyMusicStaff AND (b) measured director time savings ≥50% vs the prior paper/email process. If both the growth thesis and the roster-only test fail → stop; no unproven continuation.

## Risks / open questions
- **Director adoption remains the single point of failure.** Phase 0 tests it before the expensive build. If directors won't distribute even the concierge version, stop or re-wedge.
- **District pushback despite framing + policy check.** A district could still shut the link down later. Residual risk accepted, monitored in pilot; the policy check converts this from unknown to measured.
- **Stripe configuration assumptions.** The controller tuple's exact property names, technical behavior (sandbox), tax determination (docs-sourced), and professional review are all Phase 0 items; if any contradicts the decision record, it is re-written before any payments code.
- **Payment habit displacement.** Legacy Venmo families may stay off-platform; v1 tolerates "new students on-platform, legacy grandfathered." Leakage measured, not fought.
- **Seasonality.** Registration clusters at semester boundaries; Phase 0/v1 must target one (January or August) or accept slow numbers.
- **Free-period economics.** $0 fee + real costs is deliberately temporary; per-hub margin data + pilot pricing questions feed the commercialization decision by the expansion gate.

## Out of scope
- Academic tutoring, sports coaching, non-music verticals.
- Marketplace demand promises ("we bring you students") beyond the hub's own network.
- Video upload/processing pipeline, native mobile apps, in-platform video lessons, LMS features, scheduling engine — all post-v1.2 decisions.
- District-level procurement and district-vendor compliance path (until a district asks).
- Stored-value products of any kind — gift-a-lesson deferred indefinitely pending jurisdiction review.
- Per-lesson and auto-recurring billing (v1 is monthly teacher-issued invoices only).
- Final pricing/commercialization — decided at the expansion gate with usage + margin data.
